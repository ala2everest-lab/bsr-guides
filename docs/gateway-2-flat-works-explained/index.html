Gateway 2 for flat works, explained

By Alicja Salter, Building Surveyor and BSR compliance consultant (MSc Building Surveying) · Last reviewed 16 November 2026

If you are planning work to a flat in a higher-risk building (HRB), someone will probably tell you the job "needs to go through Gateway 2". The phrase is everywhere in the industry, but it was coined for new tall buildings, and it can make a flat refurbishment sound bigger than it is. This guide explains what the gateways are, which ones apply to work in an existing flat, and what each step involves. It is general information, not legal advice or a building control decision.

The short answer

  • "Gateway 2" is shorthand for building control approval from the Building Safety Regulator (BSR). For work to an existing HRB, you must have that approval before building work starts, unless the work is exempt, competent person scheme work, certain fibre optic cabling or an emergency repair (GOV.UK).
  • "Gateway 3" is shorthand for the completion certificate. When the work is finished, you apply to BSR for a completion certificate, and BSR inspects the work before deciding (GOV.UK).
  • Gateway 1 rarely matters for flat works. It sits at the planning stage, and most internal flat works do not need a planning application.

Where the gateways come from

The gateways were set up after the Grenfell Tower fire as three hard stops in the life of a new higher-risk building: one at planning, one before construction starts and one before people move in. At each stop, the project cannot move on until the regulator is satisfied.

For new buildings the language fits well. For work to a flat in a building that already exists, the official guidance mostly talks about "building control approval" and the "completion certificate" rather than gateways. The steps are the same in substance, so when a contractor or architect says "Gateway 2", they mean the BSR application and approval stage.

Gateway What it is Relevant to flat works?
Gateway 1 Fire safety considered at the planning application stage Rarely: most internal flat works need no planning application
Gateway 2 BSR building control approval before work starts Yes, unless the work is exempt or another exception applies
Gateway 3 BSR completion certificate when the work is finished Yes, for any work that needed approval

Gateway 2 for an existing flat: what actually happens

1. Decide whether you need approval at all

Not every job in a high-rise flat needs a BSR application. Like-for-like replacements of baths, toilets, basins and sinks, and most repairs, are exempt work. New drainage, electrical work around baths and showers, heating replacements and anything that changes the layout or affects fire safety usually is not (GOV.UK). If any part of the job needs approval, the whole job goes into the application.

2. Work out the category

BSR sorts work into Category A and Category B. Category A covers anything on a fixed list in regulation 12(6) of the Building (Higher-Risk Buildings Procedures) (England) Regulations 2023, including changes to the internal layout and work affecting passive or active fire safety measures. Category B is everything else. The category decides how many documents you submit, and GOV.UK warns that applying under the wrong one is likely to lead to rejection. See Category A or Category B? How BSR classifies work in a high-rise flat.

3. Assemble the team and the documents

The application must name a client, a principal designer and a principal contractor. For Category A work it needs a full set of documents, including drawings, a building regulations compliance statement, a construction control plan, a change control plan, a mandatory occurrence reporting plan, a competence declaration and a fire and emergency file (GOV.UK). Category B applications are lighter, although BSR can ask for more.

BSR expects evidence about the existing building, not assumptions. If information is missing, you back any assumptions with surveys or inspections and explain how you will check them during the works. See Which professionals do I need for a BSR application?

4. Validation and assessment

BSR first checks that the application is complete. A valid application is not an approved one. It then assesses the work. For work to an existing HRB, BSR has up to 8 weeks to decide, unless a longer period is agreed (GOV.UK). In practice many decisions take longer, so plan for that. See How long does BSR approval take?

5. The decision

BSR can approve, approve with requirements or reject. If it approves with requirements, you can only go ahead with the parts the requirements do not affect. If it rejects, you must not start. You can ask BSR to review a decision and then appeal to the First-tier Tribunal.

Approval is not quite the end of Gateway 2. Before work begins you must give a notice of intention to start at least 5 working days ahead, and a notice of commencement within 5 working days of reaching commencement (GOV.UK). If the work has not reached commencement within 3 years, the approval lapses.

Between the gateways: change control

During the works, you must build what was approved. Every change goes in a change control log. Notifiable changes must be reported to BSR before the related work starts, and major changes need BSR approval before that part of the work goes ahead. BSR has up to 6 weeks to decide a major change application unless a longer period is agreed (GOV.UK).

Gateway 3 for an existing flat: the completion certificate

When the work is finished, you apply for a full completion certificate. The application includes the latest drawings, the control plans, the compliance statement, the fire and emergency file, a summary of the completed work and the change control log. It also needs a signed statement from the client that information about the work has been handed to the building's accountable person (GOV.UK). BSR inspects the work and reviews the records before deciding. See After BSR approval: notices, changes and the completion certificate for flat works.

Common misunderstandings

  • "Gateway 2 is only for new buildings." The term comes from new-build, but the approval it describes applies to building work in existing HRBs too.
  • "Our contractor's building control body can sign it off." For HRBs, BSR is the building control authority. Local authority building control and private approvers cannot approve the work.
  • "We'll apply and start while we wait." Starting work that needs approval before you have it is a criminal offence. See What happens if I start work without BSR approval?
  • "The rules are about to change, so we can plan on Category B." The government is minded to take most work inside flats out of Category A, but as of the date of this guide no new regulations have been made. Plan on the current rules.

A quick checklist

  1. Confirm the building is higher-risk: at least 18 metres or 7 storeys, with at least two residential units.
  2. List every item of work and sort it: not building work, exempt, scheme work, or needs approval.
  3. If anything needs approval, choose the category and appoint your principal designer and principal contractor.
  4. Prepare and submit the application, and build the decision time into your programme.
  5. Give the start notices, keep the change control log and apply for the completion certificate at the end.
